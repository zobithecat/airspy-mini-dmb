\begin{abstract}
Terrestrial Digital Multimedia Broadcasting (T-DMB) has been in continuous
operation in the Republic of Korea since December 2005, yet two decades on
there exists no \emph{complete} open-source software stack that takes a
T-DMB RF signal from a low-cost software-defined radio all the way to a
decoded video frame.  Prior efforts fall into two camps: (i) DAB receivers
such as \texttt{welle.io} and \texttt{eti-cmdline} that stop after producing
Ensemble Transport Interface (ETI) frames, and (ii) the recent GNU Radio +
patched FFmpeg pipeline of Eo and Bahk~\cite{eo2024tdmb} which decodes
audio/video but is deeply tied to GNU Radio's graphical companion and a set
of out-of-tree patches.  This paper presents a third path: a self-contained
Python package implementing the full T-DMB receive chain --- ETI parsing,
Fast Information Channel (FIC) decoding, Reed-Solomon (RS) plus
convolutional time-deinterleaver outer FEC, MPEG-4 Sync Layer (SL) packet
demultiplexing, and H.264 elementary-stream reconstruction --- with the
final video decoding stage delegated to an unmodified build of the
\texttt{dmb-oss} FFmpeg fork.  We verify the chain on a 30\,MB
on-air capture of YTN DMB (channel K8B, 183.008\,MHz) using an
Airspy~Mini~\cite{airspy} and a consumer indoor antenna: 87.3\% of
Reed-Solomon blocks decode cleanly, every SL packet header parses, and
ffmpeg renders QVGA H.264 Baseline video frames containing recognisable
Korean broadcast content.  We document, in code, the
sync-byte-aligned deinterleaver path that the conventional
``deinterleave-then-find-sync'' ordering fails to recover, and we
record an empirical observation that the YTN DMB multiplex on K8B
embeds the AVC \texttt{DecoderConfigurationRecord} inside the
Scene-Description / BIFS stream rather than the
Object-Descriptor stream used in the reference MPEG-4 Systems
examples --- an operator configuration choice permitted by
TS~102\,428 and already catalogued by ETRI in
2004~\cite{lee2004m4smux}.
The code, captures, and per-capture metadata sidecars are released on
GitHub.  Beyond the engineering documentation, we explicitly position
the artefact as a graduate-level term project that, in one assignment,
integrates DSP, RF, broadcasting systems, channel coding, and video
codec topics --- the open-source SDR-based successor to the C++
T-DMB educational simulator of Hwang and Kim
(2015)~\cite{hwang2015simulator}.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
Software-defined radio, T-DMB, DAB, MPEG-4 SL packets, Reed-Solomon,
convolutional interleaver, open source, engineering education.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Korean Terrestrial DMB extends the European Eureka-147 DAB physical
layer~\cite{etsi300401} with an additional Reed-Solomon outer code and a
Forney convolutional time interleaver, in order to deliver MPEG-4
AVC~/~H.264 video and MPEG-4 BSAC audio over MPEG-2 transport streams
multiplexed into DAB sub-channels~\cite{ttak0026,etsi102427,etsi102428}.
The service has been in commercial operation since December 2005, was
deployed nationwide, and remains in service in 2026 despite the rise of
smartphone streaming.  Its specifications are publicly available, the
modulation is the same DAB Mode~I OFDM used across Europe, and dedicated
DAB demodulators exist as open source (notably \texttt{welle.io}, the
\texttt{gr-dab} GNU Radio out-of-tree module, and Jan van Katwijk's
\texttt{eti-stuff}).  In spite of this, until very recently no published
software released under a free licence has been able to take a T-DMB RF
capture and produce a playable video file end-to-end without proprietary
tools.

The closest precedent is the recent ICTC paper by
Eo and Bahk~\cite{eo2024tdmb} (with their \texttt{dmb-oss} repository),
which demonstrated playback by chaining \texttt{gr-dab} with a patched
FFmpeg.  Their work is the canonical reference for the Korean T-DMB
encoding chain and we rely on it heavily.  However the released artefact
is a GNU~Radio flowgraph plus a set of FFmpeg patches; it requires the
graphical GNU~Radio Companion, has not been packaged for headless or
library use, and reports several practical gotchas (alignment of the
deinterleaver, missing composition timestamps) without
all-in-one tooling to mitigate them.

This paper describes an independent re-implementation with three goals:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Library-first.}  Every stage is a Python module
       (\texttt{tdmb.eti}, \texttt{tdmb.fec}, \texttt{tdmb.msc}, ...) with a
       documented API, so individual layers can be unit-tested and reused
       (e.g.\ a pure-Python Forney deinterleaver that decodes the same
       reference capture as our pipeline in under a minute).
\item \textbf{Reproducible captures.}  Every RF capture writes a JSON
       sidecar recording the channel, frequency, sample rate, gain stages,
       bias-tee state, antenna model, and capture location.  We retro-fit
       sidecars for our reference captures so that the artefact bundle is
       self-describing.
\item \textbf{One-shot end-to-end driver.}  A single command
       (\texttt{python tools/decode\_video.py <capture>.eti}) traverses
       the full chain and emits an MP4 plus IDR thumbnail PNGs.  No GUI
       and no FFmpeg patches at run time are required (we link against a
       \emph{pre-built} unmodified \texttt{dmb-oss} FFmpeg for the final
       H.264~/~BSAC decode).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope, contributions, and intended audience}
This paper is an \emph{engineering and reproducibility} contribution
rather than a contribution to signal-processing theory or to the
T-DMB standard itself.  Every individual element documented in
Sections~\ref{sec:rs}--\ref{sec:avcc} below --- the Reed-Solomon
parameters, the Forney interleaver geometry, the SL packet header
layout, the location of the AVC decoder configuration record ---
is fully specified by publicly-available standards (ETSI
TS~102\,427/428~\cite{etsi102427,etsi102428},
TTAK.KO-07.0026~\cite{ttak0026}, ISO/IEC
14496-1~\cite{iso14496-1}, ISO/IEC 13818-1
Amendment~7~\cite{iso13818-1-amd7}) and was already catalogued
in 2004 by ETRI~\cite{lee2004m4smux}, whose paper enumerates
exactly the ``MPEG-2 TS + MPEG-4 SL per ISO/IEC 13818-1 Amendment
7'' path that the deployed Korean T-DMB stack uses today (and an
unadopted alternative multiplexer, M4SMux, in the same paper).
A reader who already has access to the engineering documents
behind a T-DMB hardware decoder, or who has read
Lee~et~al.~\cite{lee2004m4smux} carefully, will find few
technical surprises in the sections that follow.

The contribution of this paper is twofold.  First, we made the
deployed receive chain available as a single, library-form,
end-to-end software artefact in a contemporary language and
runtime, with reproducibility scaffolding (per-capture JSON
sidecars, a public reference RF capture released under CC-BY) that
to the best of our knowledge has not been previously released in
the open literature.  Second, we did this in a \emph{black-box}
posture --- treating Korean T-DMB as an unannotated bitstream and
deriving every layer empirically from a single 30\,MB on-air
capture --- and along the way independently rediscovered several
operator-specific deployment choices that are documented in the
Korean-language ETRI literature but not, to our knowledge, in
English.  This black-box angle is what makes the artefact
educationally useful (Section~\ref{sec:education}): a student can
walk the same path without prior access to the standards
ecosystem.  Concretely:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item A pure-Python implementation of the T-DMB outer FEC chain
      (RS(204,188) over $GF(2^8)$ with DVB-T polynomial plus a
      $12\times17$ Forney deinterleaver) that achieves 87.3\% block
      success on a reference Korean K8B capture (75\% FIB CRC pass
      rate, complete ensemble recovery).
\item An open-source Python implementation of the sync-byte-aligned
      Forney deinterleaver path noted by Eo and
      Bahk~\cite{eo2024tdmb} as the cause of failed RS decoding in
      naive implementations.  Section~\ref{sec:rs} reports a clean
      0\% $\to$ 87.3\% before/after comparison on a single reference
      capture.
\item An empirical, code-form re-derivation of the specific
      MPEG-4~Systems SLConfigDescriptor profile that Korean
      operators have settled on
      (\texttt{useAccessUnitStartFlag}=1, \texttt{useAccessUnitEndFlag}=1,
      \texttt{useTimeStampsFlag}=1,
      \texttt{timeStampLength}=33,
      \texttt{accessUnitDuration}=0), under which every SL packet
      header is exactly 9 bytes with byte~0 fixed at \texttt{0xCF}.
      This profile is permitted by ISO/IEC
      14496-1~\cite{iso14496-1} and matches the framework
      catalogued by Lee~et~al.~\cite{lee2004m4smux}; what we
      contribute is a verified ${>}3000$-PES black-box
      reconstruction of the exact field values, which to our
      knowledge has not been published in English.
\item An open-source locator and parser for the AVC
      \texttt{DecoderConfigurationRecord}, which the YTN DMB
      multiplex on K8B embeds inside the Scene-Description
      (\texttt{tid}=\texttt{0x05}) section stream rather than the
      Object-Descriptor (\texttt{tid}=\texttt{0x04}) stream used
      in the reference MPEG-4 Systems
      examples~\cite{iso14496-1}.  This is an operator
      configuration choice permitted by TS~102\,428's allowance
      of stream-type restrictions~\cite{etsi102428}, not a
      standards deviation.
\item Release of an Overleaf-ready paper source (including a
      Korean translation), the Python package, a 30\,MB reference
      capture with its metadata sidecar, and twenty decoded IDR
      thumbnails from that capture.
\item A turnkey graduate-level term-project artefact that
      integrates DSP, RF, broadcasting, channel coding, and codec
      topics into a single hands-on assignment
      (Section~\ref{sec:education}).  Note that the educational
      value flows directly from the black-box framing: the project
      is interesting precisely because the standards are publicly
      available but the deployment specifics
      (cf.~\cite{lee2004m4smux}) require empirical reconstruction.
\end{itemize}

The primary intended audience is therefore not researchers
seeking novel signal-processing results, but: (i)~engineers and
students who want to study or extend a working T-DMB receive
chain end-to-end without commercial test equipment;
(ii)~educators looking for an integrated hands-on project for a
graduate-level signals / communications / broadcasting course;
and (iii)~the broader open-source SDR community, for whom Korean
T-DMB has until now had no library-form receiver.

\section{Outer FEC: Sync-Aligned Deinterleaving}\label{sec:rs}

T-DMB augments the DAB physical layer with an outer protection step
specified in ETSI TS~102\,427~\cite{etsi102427}: each 188-byte MPEG-2
transport-stream packet is first Reed-Solomon encoded into a
204-byte codeword (RS(204,188) shortened from RS(255,239), with
$\alpha=2$, primitive polynomial $0x11d$, FCR$=0$ -- identical to
DVB-T), and the resulting codeword stream is then byte-wise
convolutional-interleaved using a Forney scheme with $N=12$ branches
and depth $M=17$.

At the receiver, after Viterbi inner-FEC decoding and energy
descrambling we obtain a stream of MSC sub-channel bytes.  A naive
implementation runs the Forney deinterleaver immediately, then scans
the deinterleaved output for the 204-byte alignment of the TS sync
byte~(\texttt{0x47}).  On our reference K8B capture this approach
recovered \emph{zero} successful Reed-Solomon blocks out of 25\,953
attempts.

The cause is that the conv-interleaver branches are indexed modulo
$N=12$ based on stream position.  Byte~0 of every transmit-side RS
block goes through branch~0 (zero delay) and so emerges at transmit
positions $0, 204, 408, \ldots$ --- but the very first MSC byte we
\emph{observe} in our capture is not byte~0 of any RS block; it is
some arbitrary phase $\phi$ within the 204-byte cycle.  If we feed that
byte into the receive-side deinterleaver as ``branch~0,'' the branch
indices are off by $\phi \bmod 12$ for the entire stream, and the
deinterleaver permutes every byte to the wrong slot.  Crucially the
0x47 sync byte still emerges at the same cadence (because every
$204$-byte block also has its first byte on branch~0 of the receive
deinterleaver, by coincidence of $204=N\cdot M$), so a sync-scan
\emph{after} deinterleaving cannot distinguish the misaligned case
from the aligned one.  This matches an observation in passing by
Eo and Bahk~\cite{eo2024tdmb}: ``the deinterleaver requires the sync
byte of a TS packet to be the first in an input byte chunk.''

Our fix is to perform sync detection \emph{before} the deinterleaver.
We accumulate $\sim$50 RS blocks ($\sim$10\,kB) of raw MSC bytes,
compute the count of \texttt{0x47} occurrences at each of 204 candidate
phases, and require the best phase to be (a)~hit by $\geq 40\%$ of
blocks and (b)~$\geq 5\times$ the second-best phase.  Once locked, we
discard the bytes preceding the chosen phase and feed the remainder
into a freshly-constructed Forney deinterleaver.  After the
$(N{-}1)\cdot M\cdot N = 2244$-byte settling transient the
deinterleaver emits 204-byte RS codewords ready for systematic
correction.

Figure~\ref{fig:phasehist} plots, for the reference K8B capture,
the count of \texttt{0x47}-at-phase-$\phi$ occurrences across all
25{,}953 candidate 204-byte windows.  A single phase
($\phi^*=160$) accounts for 96.3\% of all hits; every other phase
sits in the noise floor below 0.6\%.  The selectivity is so
sharp because the TS sync byte is the only byte whose value is
fixed across all RS blocks, and the conv interleaver preserves
that determinism at the corresponding phase.

\section{SL Demultiplexing}\label{sec:sl}

The transport stream emerging from the RS decoder carries four logical
PIDs (Table~\ref{tab:pids}).  PID~\texttt{0x113} contains the video
elementary stream packetised first into MPEG-4 SL packets and then
into MPEG-2 PES with \texttt{stream\_id} = \texttt{0xFA}
(``MPEG-4 SL-packetised stream''), per ISO/IEC 13818-1 Amendment
7~\cite{iso13818-1-amd7}.  Lee~et~al.~\cite{lee2004m4smux} document
this same encapsulation as the ``new DMB approach using the MPEG-4
system'' that was being researched at ETRI at the time and
ultimately won out over their proposed M4SMux alternative.
Each PES on PID~\texttt{0x113} carries exactly one SL packet, and
each SL packet contains exactly one H.264 NAL unit (without
start-code prefix).

\subsection{SL header layout}

ISO/IEC 14496-1~\cite{iso14496-1} defines \texttt{SLPacketHeader()}
as a generic syntax whose actual on-wire fields are governed by a
per-stream \texttt{SLConfigDescriptor}.  In a black-box setting we
do not see the \texttt{SLConfigDescriptor} directly (it would arrive
in the Initial Object Descriptor; we recover the IOD only
indirectly through trial parsing), so we instead solve for the
profile that makes the bitstream consistent.  Combining
ISO/IEC~14496-1's degrees of freedom with the FFmpeg
\texttt{read\_sl\_header()} implementation in \texttt{dmb-oss} and
the framework catalogue in
Lee~et~al.~\cite{lee2004m4smux}, the maximum-length AU-start
header for an SL packet carrying a 33-bit DTS and a 33-bit CTS
contains:

\begin{align*}
\text{header bits} \;=\; & 1 \;(\text{au\_start}) + 1 \;(\text{au\_end}) + 1 \;(\text{padding}) + \\
                         & 1 \;(\text{rand\_acc\_pt}) + 1 \;(\text{dts\_flag}) + 1 \;(\text{cts\_flag}) + \\
                         & 33 \;(\text{DTS}) + 33 \;(\text{CTS}) \\
                       =\; & 72 \; \text{bits} \;=\; 9 \; \text{bytes exactly}.
\end{align*}

We confirm this empirically on \emph{every one} of the 3\,135 SL
packets in our reference capture: each PES body starts with the byte
\texttt{0xCF} (=$0b11001111$, decoding to
\{au\_start,au\_end\}=\{1,1\}, \{padding,rand\_acc\_pt\}=\{0,0\},
\{dts\_flag,cts\_flag\}=\{1,1\}, plus two high bits of DTS), followed
by 8 more bytes of timestamp fields, after which byte~9 is the NAL
header of a single H.264 NAL unit.  The implied
\texttt{SLConfigDescriptor} is therefore
\texttt{useAccessUnitStartFlag}=1, \texttt{useAccessUnitEndFlag}=1,
\texttt{useTimeStampsFlag}=1,
\texttt{timeStampLength}=33,
\texttt{accessUnitDuration}=0.  This is a standards-compliant
operator choice rather than a deviation: every SL packet in the
video PID is a single complete access unit with both timestamps
explicit, which is the simplest interpretation Korean operators
could have picked and is consistent with the
ISO/IEC 13818-1 Amendment 7 path catalogued at ETRI in
2004~\cite{lee2004m4smux}.  What we contribute is the empirical
${>}3000$-PES verification of the exact field
values, which to our knowledge has not been published in English.

\subsection{NAL stream}

After stripping the 9-byte SL header from every PES we obtain a stream
of bare H.264 NAL bytes.  The NAL header histogram on our reference
capture is dominated by non-IDR slices (3\,038) with periodic IDR
slices (97), giving a Group-of-Pictures (GoP) length of roughly~32 ---
typical for a 30\,fps QVGA broadcast.  No SPS or PPS NAL units are
present in the elementary stream, which is consistent with
AVCC-style storage where decoder configuration is carried out-of-band.

\section{Locating the AVC Decoder Configuration Record}
\label{sec:avcc}

In the reference MPEG-4 Systems examples given by ISO/IEC
14496-1~\cite{iso14496-1}, the H.264
\texttt{AVCDecoderConfigurationRecord} (which contains the SPS and
PPS NAL units required to bootstrap the decoder) is transported
inside the Object-Descriptor stream as part of a
\texttt{DecoderSpecificInfo} descriptor.  In the YTN DMB capture we
observe the opposite: the OD-stream sections on PID~\texttt{0x111}
contain only DMB-private commands with user-defined tags
(\texttt{0xC0}--\texttt{0xFE}, the ``Annex K user-private'' range);
the actual AVCC bytes appear inside the Scene-Description / BIFS
section stream on PID~\texttt{0x112}.  This is an operator
configuration choice, not a standards deviation: ETSI TS~102\,428
\cite{etsi102428} explicitly permits stream-type restrictions in
the DMB video-service profile, and the catalogue of permitted
descriptor placements in
Lee~et~al.~\cite{lee2004m4smux} is broad enough to accommodate it.
Without access to the operator's IOD ahead of time, however, a
clean-room decoder cannot infer the location from the standards
alone, and must locate the record by content.

Our locator scans the SD-stream byte stream for the AVCC signature
$$\texttt{01}\;\langle\textit{profile}\rangle\;\langle\textit{compat}\rangle
   \;\langle\textit{level}\rangle\;\texttt{FF}\;\texttt{E1}\;
   \langle\textit{SPS\_len}\rangle\;\langle\textit{SPS}\rangle\;
   \texttt{01}\;\langle\textit{PPS\_len}\rangle\;\langle\textit{PPS}\rangle$$
constraining profile to a known H.264 value
(\texttt{0x42}/\texttt{0x4D}/\texttt{0x58}/\texttt{0x64}/\texttt{0x6E}),
level to $[\texttt{0x09},\texttt{0x42}]$, the
\texttt{lengthSizeMinusOne}/reserved byte to the form
$\texttt{FC}|x$, and the \texttt{numOfSPS}/reserved byte to
$\texttt{E0}|n$ with $n=1$.  This signature is sufficient to
uniquely identify the record amid the surrounding BIFS commands.  On
the K8B reference capture the recovered record is:

\begin{lstlisting}
SPS (9 B):  67 42 00 0d 95 a0 50 7c 40
PPS (4 B):  68 ce 38 80
            -- profile_idc = 0x42 (Baseline)
            -- level_idc   = 0x0d (Level 1.3)
\end{lstlisting}

Prepending these as Annex-B NAL units to the extracted slice stream
produces a fully self-contained H.264 byte stream that FFmpeg
identifies as \texttt{Baseline 320x240 25\,fps yuv420p}.
Figure~\ref{fig:idr} shows two of the IDR frames recovered from
the reference capture, demonstrating that the full pipeline
indeed produces visually-coherent Korean broadcast content.

\section{Use as a graduate-level term project}\label{sec:education}

The complete artefact is, in our view, best understood as a
term-project platform for upper-undergraduate or first-year
graduate courses in signal processing, communications, or
broadcasting systems.  A single eight-week project built on this
codebase exposes a student to, in approximately one credit hour
per week:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{DSP:} sampling theorem, decimation, anti-alias
      filtering, FFT-based spectrum analysis, OFDM carriers and
      cyclic prefix.
\item \textbf{RF and SDR:} R820T2 LNA / mixer / VGA gain stages,
      noise figure, bias-tee, hardware AGC, antenna theory
      (folded dipole, Yagi, VSWR).
\item \textbf{Channel coding:} CRC-16, Viterbi inner FEC,
      Reed-Solomon (204,188) over $GF(2^8)$, Forney convolutional
      time interleaver.
\item \textbf{Broadcasting systems:} DAB Mode~I OFDM frame
      structure, FIC / MSC partitioning, ensemble / service /
      sub-channel hierarchy, EEP coding profiles.
\item \textbf{Codecs and containers:} MPEG-2 TS, PES, PMT, IOD,
      MPEG-4 SL packets, H.264 NAL units, SPS/PPS, AVCC vs
      Annex-B.
\item \textbf{Software engineering:} Python packaging, CLI tool
      design, structured logging (metadata sidecars), Git LFS for
      reference data, MIT/CC-BY licensing.
\end{itemize}

A suggested eight-week project structure is shown in
Table~\ref{tab:syllabus}.  Per-student hardware cost is
$\approx\,\$120$ (Airspy~Mini \$100, antenna materials \$10--20)
and the codebase has been verified to run on consumer macOS and
Linux laptops without GPU.  Antenna construction (week 8)
provides a natural extension into hands-on RF and is graded on
measured VSWR plus the channel-lock rate achieved with the
student-built antenna against the reference indoor antenna of
Table~\ref{tab:capture-meta}.

We note that Hwang and Kim~\cite{hwang2015simulator} presented a
C++ T-DMB frame-analysis simulator targeted at the same
educational gap in 2015.  Their work used a USB-tethered
commercial T-DMB receiver board and a Visual C++ dialog application
to display Ensemble Id, service labels, sub-channel locations
(start address, size, protection level), and to dump FIC / SubCh
raw bytes for later inspection.  The pedagogical depth of that
simulator is, by construction, bounded by what the receiver board
exposed to its USB host: \emph{the post-decoded Fast Information
Channel metadata}.  Everything below the FIC --- OFDM
demodulation, Viterbi inner FEC, Reed-Solomon + convolutional
deinterleaver outer FEC, MSC sub-channel byte extraction,
MPEG-2 TS demultiplexing, MPEG-4 SL packet unpacking, H.264 NAL
parsing, and the video codec itself --- was a black box hidden in
the board's firmware, inaccessible to a student even in principle.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The closest predecessor is Eo and Bahk~\cite{eo2024tdmb}, who use
\texttt{gr-dab} for the DAB layer and a patched FFmpeg for the
T-DMB-specific decoding, and who explicitly document several of the
gotchas we re-encountered (deinterleaver sync alignment, missing
composition timestamps, SL packet header parsing).  Their work
remains the canonical reference for the encoding chain.  By contrast
this work emphasises packaging (a single Python package with
documented API) and reproducibility (per-capture metadata sidecars,
a single one-shot decoder script, no run-time FFmpeg modification).

\texttt{welle.io}~\cite{welleio} is a high-quality cross-platform DAB
audio receiver, but does not decode the T-DMB video stream type.
\texttt{eti-cmdline}~\cite{etistuff} (from which we re-use the OFDM
and Viterbi back-end via a small patch) outputs ETI(NI) frames only
and was the starting point for our work.  An earlier portable hardware
implementation is described by Lee~et~al.~\cite{lee2007portable},
and the Korean T-DMB system as a whole is documented in
\cite{ttak0026,etsi102427,etsi102428,hwang2015simulator,iso14496-1}.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented, to the best of our knowledge, the first packaged
end-to-end open-source software receiver for Korean Terrestrial DMB.
The pipeline takes a 3\,MSPS I/Q capture from an Airspy~Mini SDR and
produces a playable H.264 video file using only freely-distributable
components.  Two implementation lessons stand out: (i)~the Forney
deinterleaver must be aligned to the TS sync byte \emph{before}
processing rather than after, and (ii)~Korean broadcasters embed the
H.264 AVCC inside the BIFS/SceneDescription stream, not the
ObjectDescriptor stream as MPEG-4 Systems prescribes.  Either lesson
silently breaks an otherwise-correct implementation.  The code,
captures, and metadata schema are released to allow further research
on T-DMB and on its now-encrypted HD~DMB successor.
